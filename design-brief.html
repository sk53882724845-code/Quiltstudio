How to write a useful design brief

A design brief is a short description of the problem a design should solve. It gives the designer context before they choose a layout, colour or visual style.

1. Explain the goal

Start with the result you want. For example, are you announcing an event, explaining a service, introducing a video or helping people recognise a project? A goal is more useful than a vague instruction such as “make it look cool”.

2. Describe the audience

Think about who will see the design and what they already know. A graphic for existing customers may use different language from one introducing a project to new visitors.

3. Name the format and placement

Say where the design will appear: a social post, story, video thumbnail, printed poster or presentation slide. Include the required dimensions or platform specification when you know it.

4. Gather the content

Provide the exact wording, dates, names, logo files and contact details that must appear. Check spelling and make sure you have permission to use supplied photographs, fonts and logos.

5. Share useful references

Show examples that communicate a mood or layout you like. Explain what you like about them. References should guide the direction, not be copied wholesale.

6. Agree on practical details

Before work begins, agree on deliverables, number of concepts, revision rounds, timeline, file formats and price. Put the agreement in writing so expectations are clear.

A simple brief outline

  • Project and goal
  • Audience
  • Platform and dimensions
  • Exact text and required assets
  • Preferred style and references
  • Deadline, deliverables and review process
This guide is general educational information. Platform requirements and project needs can change; check the relevant platform's current documentation before publishing.
